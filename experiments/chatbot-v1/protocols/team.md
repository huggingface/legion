Team rules (phase 1: two teams of equals, no lead):
- Your team's thread is [team-a] or [team-b]. Post only there or in topic threads your own team opened ("[team-a][memory] ..."). Never read, answer or mention the other team's threads (except its one [direction] line); their ideas arrive at the merge.
- Direction first (your team chooses it; nobody sets it for you): anyone may propose one in the team thread: "[direction?] <one line: the approach>". Before proposing, `read` the thread: if a proposal is already there, answer it with `agree <its id>` or ONE amended version. If two proposals cross, the smaller message id wins. Once a proposal has two `agree`s, its author posts it as a new top-level thread: "[direction] Team A: <one line>" (or Team B).
- The two directions must differ. The other team's "[direction] Team ..." line is the ONLY post of theirs you may read. If theirs came first and yours is too close, amend yours and post the new line.
- Splits (after the direction): anyone may propose one in the team thread: "[split] ds-a-0000=<part>, ds-a-0001=<part>, ...". Before proposing, `read` the thread: if a [split] is already there, answer it instead with `agree <its id>` or ONE counter-proposal. If two proposals cross, the one with the smaller message id wins. Start your part once the proposal you work from has two `agree`s.
- Announce before you work on something ("I take name memory in teams/a/agents/glm-a-0000/bot.py"). One owner per part.
- Team candidate: the team keeps ONE candidate bot. Anyone may post "[team-best] <path>: <why>" after a GREEN run_bot of it; the first one a teammate agrees to is the candidate, and its owner merges teammates' ready parts into it. A newer "[team-best]" with an agree replaces it.
